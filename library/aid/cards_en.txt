# Doba first-aid cards, English. "= Title | line under it", "# heading",
# "- step", other lines are text. Draft: to be checked by a medic before use.

= How to use these cards | read first
Short steps for the first minutes, until help comes. They do not replace training.
# Always first
- Make sure you are safe: traffic, fire, gas, water, electricity, shooting. Do not become the second casualty.
- Call 112 (or have someone call) as early as you can. 112 works across Europe; in Ukraine also 103 for an ambulance, 101 for rescue.
- Stop heavy bleeding first, then check breathing.
- Say what you do. Write down times (tourniquet, when symptoms started).
Draft: these cards have not yet been checked by a medic.

= Severe bleeding | press, pack, tourniquet
# Do now
- Call 112. Protect your hands (gloves, a plastic bag) if you can.
- Press hard, straight on the wound, with a cloth or your hands. Keep pressing; do not lift to look.
- Blood soaks through: put more cloth on top and press harder. Do not remove the first layer.
- Deep wound in the neck, armpit or groin, or bleeding that will not stop: pack the wound. Push cloth or gauze deep into it until it is full, then press hard for at least 3 minutes.
- Arm or leg, blood spurting or pooling, or pressure does not stop it: tourniquet (next card).
- Lay the person down, keep them warm, and keep checking breathing.
# Do not
- Do not pull out an object stuck in the wound: pad around it and press around it.
- Do not give food or drink.

= Tourniquet | arm or leg only
- Only on an arm or a leg.
- Put it 5 to 7 cm above the wound, not on a joint. If you cannot see the wound: high on the limb, as close to the body as you can.
- Pull the strap tight, then twist the rod until the bleeding stops. Lock the rod.
- Write the time on the tourniquet or on the forehead: "T 14:20".
- Do not loosen it and do not take it off. Only medical staff remove it.
- Still bleeding: put a second tourniquet just above the first.
- It hurts a lot. That is normal and means it works.
# No tourniquet
- Improvise: a strip of cloth at least 4 cm wide, and a stick to twist it. Never wire, string or a thin cord.
- Until then: press hard on the wound.

= CPR | 30 pushes, 2 breaths
- Safe? Shout and shake the shoulders.
- No answer and not breathing normally (gasping is not normal breathing): call 112, put the phone on speaker, send someone for an AED (defibrillator).
- Kneel beside them. Heel of one hand in the centre of the chest, the other hand on top.
- Push down 5 to 6 cm, 100 to 120 times a minute (the beat of "Stayin' Alive"). Let the chest rise fully between pushes.
- After 30 pushes: tilt the head back, lift the chin, pinch the nose, give 2 breaths of 1 second each, watching the chest rise.
- Cannot or will not give breaths: just push, without stopping.
- AED arrives: switch it on and do what it says.
- Do not stop until help takes over, the person wakes and breathes normally, or you are exhausted. Change with someone every 2 minutes.
# Child or drowning
- Give 5 breaths first, then 30 pushes and 2 breaths.
- Child: one hand (or two fingers for a baby), a third of the chest's depth.

= Unconscious, breathing | on the side
- Not responding but breathing normally: put them on their side (recovery position).
- Near arm out at a right angle, far hand under the cheek, far knee bent; roll them towards you by the knee.
- Tilt the head back so the mouth points down and can drain.
- Call 112. Check breathing every minute. Breathing stops: CPR.
- Keep them warm.
- Suspected neck or back injury (a fall, a crash): move them only to keep the airway open.

= Choking | back blows, belly thrusts
- Can cough or speak: let them cough. Stay with them.
- Cannot breathe, speak or cough: lean them forward, give up to 5 hard blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
- Still choking: stand behind, fist just above the navel, other hand on it, pull hard in and up, up to 5 times.
- Repeat 5 blows and 5 thrusts until it comes out.
- They go limp: lower them down, call 112, start CPR.
- Baby under 1 year: face down on your forearm, 5 back blows; then on the back, 5 chest pushes with two fingers. No belly thrusts.
- Pregnant or very large person: chest thrusts instead of belly thrusts.
- After belly thrusts, a doctor should check them.

= Burn | cool 20 minutes
- Stop the burning: put out flames, move away from the heat.
- Cool the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes. Not ice, not iced water. Still helps up to 3 hours later.
- Take off rings, watches and clothing near the burn, unless stuck to the skin.
- Cover loosely with cling film or a clean cloth that does not shed fluff.
- Keep the person warm (cool the burn, not the person).
# Call 112
- Burn bigger than their hand, on the face, hands, feet, groin or a joint, in the mouth or throat, from chemicals or electricity, or on a child.
# Do not
- No butter, oil, cream, toothpaste. Do not burst blisters.
- Chemical: brush off powder first, then rinse for at least 20 minutes.

= Broken bone | do not straighten
- Keep the limb still, as you found it. Do not try to straighten it.
- Open wound over the bone: cover it with a clean cloth, press around the bone, not on it.
- Splint only if you must move: pad it, and tie it to something stiff (a board, sticks, rolled newspaper) so the joint above and below cannot move.
- Check fingers or toes after tying: cold, pale, blue or numb, then loosen.
- Arm: a sling; a leg can be tied to the other leg.
- Neck or back injury possible: do not move them unless there is danger.
- Watch for shock: pale, cold, sweaty, fast pulse. Lay them down, keep warm.
- Nothing to eat or drink: they may need an operation.

= Hypothermia | cold, shivering, confused
# Signs
- Shivering, slurred speech, clumsy, confused, sleepy. Severe: shivering stops.
# Do
- Out of wind, rain and snow. Into a shelter, a car, a building.
- Wet clothes off, dry clothes on. Insulate from the ground. Cover the head.
- Warm slowly: blankets, a sleeping bag, your body against theirs. Warm sweet drinks only if fully awake.
- Handle gently and keep them lying down.
- Call 112. Not breathing normally: CPR.
# Do not
- No alcohol. Do not rub the arms and legs. No hot bath for a severe case.

= Heat stroke | hot, confused: cool now
# Signs
- Hot skin, headache, confusion, strange behaviour, fainting, fits. May have stopped sweating.
# Do
- Call 112: this kills.
- Into the shade, clothes off down to underwear.
- Cool fast: pour cool water over them and fan; cold packs or ice in the neck, armpits and groin. Into cool water if you can.
- Sips of water only if fully awake.
- Unconscious and breathing: on the side. Not breathing: CPR.
# Heat exhaustion
- Sweaty, weak, dizzy, headache, still thinking clearly: rest in the shade, drink water or rehydration solution, cool down. Gets worse: as heat stroke.

= Safe water | boil, bleach, settle
- Cloudy: let it settle, pour off the clear water, filter it through a clean cloth first.
- Boil: a full rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 2000 m). Let it cool, keep it covered.
- No fire: unscented household bleach (4 to 6 % chlorine). 2 drops per litre; 4 drops if cloudy or very cold. Stir, wait 30 minutes. It should smell slightly of chlorine; if not, add the same again and wait 15 minutes more.
- Purification tablets: as the packet says.
- Sun: clear plastic bottle up to 2 litres, clear water, 6 hours in full sun (2 days if cloudy).
- Fuel, chemicals or poison in the water: boiling does not help. Find other water.
- Store safe water covered, in a clean container; scoop out with a clean cup.
# Rehydration solution
- 1 litre of safe water, 6 level teaspoons of sugar, half a level teaspoon of salt. Small sips often.

= Carbon monoxide | out to fresh air
# Signs
- Headache, dizziness, sickness, tiredness, confusion. Several people or pets ill at once. Better outside.
# Do
- Everyone out into fresh air now. Do not go back in.
- Call 112. Unconscious: on the side. Not breathing: CPR.
# Prevent
- Never run a generator, a grill, a gas heater or a camping stove inside, in a garage or in a tent. Generators at least 6 m from windows and doors.

= Radiation or chemicals | in, stay in, listen
# Radiation
- Get inside a solid building, now. Go to the middle or the basement, away from windows.
- Close windows and doors, switch off the ventilation.
- Were outside: take off the outer clothes, put them in a bag. Wash hands, face and hair with soap and water (no conditioner).
- Stay inside until the authorities say otherwise (at least 24 hours). Listen to the radio.
- Iodine tablets only when the authorities say so.
# Chemical cloud
- Go away from the smell, across the wind.
- Chlorine is heavier than air: go up, to high floors. Ammonia is lighter: go down.
- Cover nose and mouth with a wet cloth. Close windows and doors, seal gaps with wet cloth or tape.
- Splashed: take off the clothes, rinse the skin and eyes with plenty of water for 20 minutes.

= Calling for help | 112, three of anything
- 112: emergency number across Europe and in Ukraine. Say where you are first (the MGRS from Position), then what happened.
- Doba: SOS on the home page, hold 5 seconds. It is sent over the radio with your position and repeated every 5 minutes.
- Three of anything means distress: 3 whistle blasts, 3 flashes, 3 shouts, 3 fires in a triangle. Repeat every minute.
- Morse SOS: three short, three long, three short.
- On the ground, big (3 m) and contrasting: V = need help, X = need medical help, an arrow = we went this way.
- To an aircraft: both arms up (a Y) = yes, we need help. One arm up, one down = no.
- Stay where you are if people know your route: it is easier to find you.
